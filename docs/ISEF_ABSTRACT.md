# ISEF Abstract

BK polyomavirus (BKPyV) reactivation causes graft-damaging nephropathy
in immunosuppressed kidney-transplant recipients, yet management is
still reactive: clinicians watch viral loads rise, then reduce
immunosuppression and hope. This project asks whether a mechanistic
virtual cell can instead *predict* — onset, clearance, and the optimal
intervention — and whether its predictions survive contact with real
patient data. A 23-compartment ordinary-differential-equation model of
BKPyV infection in renal tubular epithelial and urothelial cells
couples intracellular replication (T-antigen gated by host S-phase),
adaptive T-cell control, plasma–urine trafficking, cell-to-cell spread,
a latent reservoir, and pharmacokinetic dosing of tacrolimus and
sirolimus. The model generates eight falsifiable signatures: a
viremia-onset *distribution* peaking at the clinically observed 4–16
weeks, per-patient clearance forecasts from three early measurements
(median prediction error <2 days on a virtual cohort, with 90%
credible intervals), a derived (not searched) optimal policy —
immediate tacrolimus-to-sirolimus conversion — and plasma-over-urine
enrichment of rearranged NCCR genomes matching published clinical
observation. Fitted to digitized longitudinal plasma series from six
transplant patients (Funk et al. 2008), the model's honest holdout
predictions beat a per-patient baseline (mean absolute error 0.82 vs
1.36 log10 copies/mL) on five of six, and failure cases are reported
with their structural causes. The result is a validated in-silico
framework for timing and choosing immunosuppression reduction — a
digital-twin path toward preemptive, personalized BKPyV management.
